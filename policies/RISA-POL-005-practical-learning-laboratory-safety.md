## 1. Hands-on teaching model

RISA's principle is “Where real learning starts from hands-on work.” Use setup-first delivery: prepare approved hardware/software and access, verify readiness, explain concepts, demonstrate, practise in an isolated or supervised setting, then assess independently. Readiness checks are not automatically summative. Provide equivalent accessible environments and remediation where appropriate.

## 2. Practical specification

Every required lab identifies objective, prerequisites, equipment/software versions, authorised environment, instructions, safe failure cases where relevant, expected outputs, raw evidence, assessment criteria, cleanup and support. Assess both outcome and interpretation; use reproducibility, error/uncertainty analysis, troubleshooting and verification. Use actual supervised experiments when physical competencies cannot be demonstrated by simulation alone.

## 3. Safety and scope

- Electronics: supervised low-voltage current-limited supplies; no unapproved mains work.
- Optics: appropriate controlled optical equipment, laser safety and supervision.
- Thermal: avoid uncontrolled heating or pressure; appropriate equipment and supervision.
- Nuclear: public datasets/simulation or appropriately authorised facilities and qualified supervision; no unauthorised source handling or unsafe experiments.
- Computing/DevOps/cybersecurity: isolated authorised environments, least privilege, no production or third-party systems without permission, protect secrets, set resource/cost limits and verify cleanup/recovery.
- Research: approve feasible question, ethics and risk assessment before data collection; require documented provenance and reproducibility.

A failed mandatory safety gate blocks practical credit until reviewed and remediated, regardless of aggregate grade. Never require unsafe failure demonstrations.

## 4. Accessibility and review

Provide validated alternative hardware/remote labs where learning outcomes are equivalent. Document reasonable accommodations. Instructors confirm equipment, permissions, supervision, incident reporting and emergency arrangements before delivery. Course-specific procedures, risk assessments and approved lab rubrics are prerequisites to deployment.
