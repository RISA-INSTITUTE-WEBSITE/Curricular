## 1. Confirmed institutional architecture

School → Course (including named career pathways) → independent Module → Topic. Sections are internal topic groupings, not an additional institutional ownership tier. A module can be linked to more than one course or school; use stable module identifiers and a shared authoritative syllabus. Do not duplicate shared content or create new codes merely for a different course.

The school allocations discussed in the curriculum design are Computing and Information Technology (Computer Science, Cloud & DevOps, Ethical Hacking); Physical and Applied Sciences (Applied Physics, Materials Science & Metallurgy); Engineering and Technology (Electronics). Course naming and register changes require explicit review.

## 2. Module and topic standards

Each syllabus specifies code/title, level, prerequisites/co-requisites, ordered sections/topics, core/elective/revision classifications, measurable outcomes, applied learning, equipment and software, assessment plan, references, version and review status. Retain code stability and traceable migration of renamed topics or modules. Verify prerequisites at topic level so parallel teaching does not create circular hard prerequisites.

Review curricula for foundational breadth, industry/scientific relevance, accessibility, unnecessary duplication, mathematical feasibility, learning hours and credit equivalence. Teach essential methods before dependent applications. Integrate computing and practical work where appropriate; preserve distinct competencies for genuinely specialised modules.

## 3. Change management

Use the Curricular repository as the source of truth. Document change rationale and impact, create a branch, submit a pull request, review for academic coherence, safety and dependencies, record approval and version/effective date, then merge. Keep decisions and change history. Merging does not itself certify academic approval or accreditation. Do not change live learning records or website curriculum without a separate implementation/migration plan. Freeze learner curriculum version or apply a documented transition with no silent loss of progress.

## 4. Adoption gate

Before delivery: review measurable outcomes, practical resources, workload and credits, assessment quality, safe delivery, and learner progression. Benchmark and review as appropriate; do not claim external recognition without evidence. Formal appointments, review cadence and credit approval remain institutional decisions.
